Keeper Information

The town of Innsmouth is a strange place. New England society
has done its level best to ignore and forget this decrepit seaside
village. Once a thriving port town, these days the harbor is full of
silt, and only local fishing boats come and go with the tide. The
train connection was abandoned not long after it was opened,
the operators calling it the ‘least profitable line’ they had ever
serviced. No one wanted to go to Innsmouth. And no one ever
seemed to leave.
Back in the 1830s, Captain Obed Marsh of Innsmouth made a
deal with the creatures that live below the Devil Reef, a dangerous
set of shallows offshore from the harbor. The creatures supplied
him with golden-alloy trinkets in exchange for glass and rubber
jewelry. Marsh smelted down the trinkets and sold the pure gold,
bringing wealth into the town and making the Marsh family
rich and powerful. Those who benefited from the gold trade
started a church they called the ‘Esoteric Order of Dagon’, a cult
worshiping Dagon, god of the sea. The Esoteric Order slowly
conquered the town of Innsmouth, driving out or converting
everyone. But the embattled authorities fought back, jailing
Obed Marsh and his disciples. The creatures, known as Deep
Ones, then moved in to protect their worshipers, killing local
law enforcement, and cementing Obed Marsh as the unspoken
leader of the town of Innsmouth. This support came at a cost,
and the members of the order were forced to take three oaths:
The First Oath: “Iä! Dagon! I, (speaker’s name), do solemnly
swear that I will neither interfere with, nor inform upon, the
activities of the Deep Ones. Iä! Dagon!”
 The Second Oath: “Iä! Dagon! I, (speaker’s name) do
solemnly swear that, if requested to do so, I will render aid to
the Deep Ones to the best of my ability, and in whatever form
or manner is required of me. Iä! Dagon!”
 The Third Oath: “Iä! Dagon! I, (speaker’s name) do solemnly
swear that I take this entity [a Deep One] as my wife/husband,
that I shall take them to my home, and that with them I will
endeavor to have and raise children, so that the race, and the
faith, can continue to prosper. Iä! Dagon! Iä! Hydra!”
And so, with the third oath, Hybrids were born—offspring
from the union between Deep One and human. Hybrids are
born looking entirely human, but as they reach adulthood and
mature, they begin to take on Deep One traits, such as enlarged
eyes, webbed fingers and other markers of their heritage. This
tends to be called the ‘Innsmouth look’. Eventually, most of the
Hybrids become able to breathe underwater, and they slip into
the sea and join their people down in the depths below.
Ginny Dubois (Born Ginny Brewster, page 4) is one such
child. Precocious and unreasonable, as many children are, Ginny
ran away to study theatre. In doing so, she set a new standard in
outrageously awful acting. Ginny is the prototypical starlet: she
bought her way into various roles using stolen family money,
but eventually even that wasn’t enough to convince Directors to
let her anywhere near a paying audience.
It is now 1923, and Ginny has struck luck once more. She is to
play the leading role in an experimental new film, The Innsmouth
Terror, and it’s going to make her a star! Sure, she has to go back
home for filming, but she’s made a few adjustments to the script
to poke fun at her backwards little village and their backwards
ways.
When a Deep One artifact turned up on set, Ginny was asked
to wear it as part of her costume. Disgusted by the idea, Ginny
stole it and hid it in her old bedroom in Innsmouth.
Ginny doesn’t know that the Director of the film, P.A.
Bazzledon (page 4) is himself in league with Mother Hydra.
He hasn’t hired Ginny for her looks, her money, or her talent.
He has hired her to be sacrificed to a Star Spawn of the god
emperor Cthulhu in front of the citizenry of Innsmouth, to be an
unwitting stooge in a terrifying ritual, and to let all of Innsmouth
know the fate of those that would betray their oaths.
