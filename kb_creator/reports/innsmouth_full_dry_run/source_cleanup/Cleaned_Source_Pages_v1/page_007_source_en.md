THE INNSMOUTH TERROR

Technology is changing rapidly! Moving pictures are the latest
big thing and there is a rush to develop color in film. While postproduction coloring has been around for a while, the dream has
always been to capture color in real time. In 1923, technicolor
refers to a prism beam-splitting system, where two black and
white films are recorded simultaneously, one behind a red filter,
and another behind a green filter. The process is complex, prone
to slipping out of alignment and, at best, finicky. Production
constantly must stop while the engineer fixes and tinkers with
the single camera capable of this feat.

The working title of this movie is The Innsmouth Terror. In
the story, a young girl (played by Ginny Dubois) is fleeing from
an abusive family. She then finds herself captured by evil froglike creatures whilst running dramatically across a field. The
frog-like men trap her inside a cave and taunt her. They then
attempt to feed her to a giant tentacled monster. Luckily, the
girl is saved at the last minute by an ingenious young man in a
bowler hat, who throws a packet of Tum-Eez, an antacid, down
the monster’s throat.

Many of the finer details of the movie, such as the appearance of
the frog-like men, have been adapted by Ginny to be particularly
offensive towards Innsmouth locals, who have noticed that the
cheaply-made costumes of the frog-men bear a not-so-striking
resemblance to the Deep Ones.

During the scenario, production moves chronologically
through the movie:

- When the investigators arrive, filming is taking place
across a dramatic field beside the train tracks, just outside
of Innsmouth.

- During the investigation, filming moves into the caves of
Innsmouth, where the prison scene is being shot.

- In the finale, filming has moved to a cliff directly above
Innsmouth, where the Star Spawn is summoned in full
view of the citizenry.

Keeper note: The Innsmouth Terror isn’t going to be a

‘Talkie’. It’s a silent film. The drive for color and sound in

film all happened around the same time, but both at once?

Madness.

INVOLVING THE INVESTIGATORS
The investigators receive a letter from the Vice Chancellor of
Miskatonic University informing them that the Orne Library,
the main library of the University, loaned out one of its treasured
artifacts to Bazzledon Productions for use in an experimental
film. However, while onsite in the seaside town of Innsmouth,
the production company has lost the artifact and suspects theft.
The investigators have been tasked with recovering the jewelry
and bringing back any evidence of the culprit so that it can be
handed over to the local constabulary (Handout 1-a: letter).
The Innsmouth Terror assumes that the investigators are
members of the Society for the Exploration of the Unexplained
(SEU) and are students or employees of the Miskatonic
University. However, they could equally be professionals or
another group hired to work for the University; the Keeper
simply needs to use Handout 1-b (letter).
Keeper note: the Society for the Exploration of the
Unexplained (SEU) is informal society made up of students
and professors of the Miskatonic University. This society
is established in the Call of Cthulhu Starter Set. Each time
the SEU finds an interesting avenue of exploration, they
choose the members that seem most capable of tackling the
mystery. The SEU meets once a month in a private reading
room of the Orne Library.
