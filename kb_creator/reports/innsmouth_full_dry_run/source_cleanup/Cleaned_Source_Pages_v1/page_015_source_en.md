It is getting late when the investigators arrive in town. As the
bus rolls in, a distinctive fishy odor overwhelms their senses.
The smell is unpleasant and ubiquitous in the center of town,
getting worse as they slowly approach the harbour. It mixes in
with the seaside breeze that is constantly blowing through the
town. The streetlights aren’t on and the night is only lit by the
orange-lamp headlights of the bus. The thundering sound of
multiple waterfalls is audible throughout the town, masking
the queer sounds that emanate from many of the dark, ruined
houses.
The investigators could not telephone ahead to book rooms in
a hotel, as Innsmouth does not have a phone line. The bus driver
croaks quietly to them that there are always rooms available
nearby in The Gilman House Hotel (page 12), and he points
at a building just off the square. Light falls out into the street
from a downstairs window, and the front doors are open. If one
were to squint slightly, it would almost appear inviting.
Investigators can stay at the Gilman House Hotel, or they spot
a firelight at the edge of town. This is the Production Train
Car (page 12), where Anna Díaz will find beds for them.
ARRIVAL
The investigators can take the 9pm coach to Innsmouth from
Dyer Street. It costs 40c and takes a little over 45 minutes. The
coach itself is rickety and travels slowly, however, it drops them
off in New Town Square, Innsmouth. Investigators with a car
may instead choose to travel there in style. However, the road to
Innsmouth is small, bumpy, and badly maintained. The driver
should pass a Drive Auto roll or find they were driving too fast,
blowing out the rear axle on the way to Innsmouth. They are
then forced to hitch a ride with the bus anyway.
Keeper note: it is better that the investigators don’t have
a car as this will limit their ability to remove themselves
from the situation in the town.
