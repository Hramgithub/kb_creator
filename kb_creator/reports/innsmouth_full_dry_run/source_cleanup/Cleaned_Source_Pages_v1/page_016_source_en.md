THE GILMAN HOUSE HOTEL
The Gilman House is the infamous hotel from The Shadow over
Innsmouth by H.P. Lovecraft. Some groups may beeline straight
there, anticipating the horror, while others will do their utmost
to avoid it.
Regardless, this once upmarket hotel was built in the 1830s
and is now showing its age. Creaking floorboards and dusty
carpets greet the investigators; flickering gas lights illuminate
the reception. Behind the desk, a pleasant, avuncular looking
receptionist takes their names and assigns them the ‘freshest’
rooms on the top floor. He comments in a smiling way that
they generally use the top floors for ‘out of towners,’ as they
are quieter and the ‘odor’ is ‘less overt’. He knows it takes some
getting used to.
The hotel lacks any semblance of a concierge service and the
receptionist’s advanced age tells the investigators that they will
have to carry their own luggage up the creaking wooden steps
to the top floor in the darkness. The receptionist hands them a
spare lantern if they think to ask for it. He informs them there
will be a lantern in each room and a ‘bedpan’ under each bed.
(The actual toilets are at the far end of the hallway).
As the investigators wander up the stairs, they should all make
Listen rolls. Successful rolls alert the investigators to an array
of odd wheezes, croaks, gasps, and one stifled scream coming
from the lower floors of the hotel. This prompts a Sanity roll
(0/1 loss) from whomever hears the noises.
When they enter their rooms, they find a no-nonsense,
though slightly dated, hotel room. A pail of fresh water sits by
the window, there’s a small writing desk, and a single bed. The
investigators’ rooms are not beside each other, spread out across
the building. The bolt locks on their rooms are flimsy, almost
pointless.
On a successful Spot Hidden roll, investigators notice that
each of their rooms has a connecting door to the one beside it So
two rooms (or more) could be connected to create a small suite.
A more paranoid investigator may realize that someone could
easily enter their room even if they have their door bolted and
barred.
It is at this point that many investigators may choose to
flee the Gilman House Hotel. Nothing bars their way as they
make their apologies and leave. If they do stay the night, the
investigator with the lowest Luck wakes around 3am to the
sound of someone attempting to open their door. If they took no
precautions and didn’t bar the door, instead they hear the door
softly closing, as if someone has been watching them sleep.

THE PRODUCTION TRAIN CAR
On the edge of Innsmouth, to the north-west, a firelight glows.
It is the only point of light visible from sleepy Innsmouth in the
dark—most of the windows are covered up by thick curtains to
keep the light (and sound) in. Investigators following the light
can quickly pick up the abandoned train track and follow it to
the Production Train Car.
Investigators may arrive at the Production Train late in the
evening or early the next morning after spending a disturbing
sleepless night in the Gilman House Hotel.
If they arrive in the evening, then they find various crew
members sitting around a fire sharing stories, drinks, and
laughing together. One of the crew, a well-built woman, stands
up to greet them. This is Anna Díaz, the producer. She introduces
herself but quickly packs them off to one of the sleeper cars to
get some rest, promising to answer all questions in the morning.
They are accommodated in a small, but surprisingly comfortable
room in the train car, with enough beds for the entire group.
They fall asleep to the sounds of laughter and merriment on the
edge of their hearing.
