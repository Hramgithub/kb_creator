- Investigators trying to get into the mind of Bazzledon
may use a Psychology or Psychoanalysis while
looking around his room. On a success, they see the
disrepair and disorganization in his private affairs,
and compare it to the detail and care he takes in his
appearance and his make-up. They note that this is a
man who cares about how he is perceived, caring for his
makeup supplies with the same care as one would see
from an artisan caring for his tools.
- Investigators interested in the makeup may attempt
a Disguise roll to realize that these are the tools of
someone familiar with changing their appearance
regularly, and not just those of a showman who likes to
look good.
Eventually, the investigators notice the locked floor safe
hidden under a creaky floorboard. Breaking in requires a
successful Lockpick roll. They could also unscrew it with a
successful Mechanical Repair roll. Inside, the investigators
uncover $4000 in cash, as well as an old Derringer handgun.
But no tiara.
Investigators can conduct a thorough search of Bazzledon’s
office and come away with no evidence that he has taken the
tiara. They also find no evidence connecting him to Mother
Hydra, as he receives instructions in his dreams and never
writes any of it down.
AROUND THE TRAIN
Set beside the sleeper car, attached to the antiquated steam
engine, a diner car has been hastily converted into a number
of large spaces. Entering from the steam engine side leads to
Bazzledon’s Office (below), the center leads into Ginny’s
Trailer (page 15), and the far side has a small ramp built into
it that leads into McGuire’s Lab (page 16). All three doors are
locked, requiring keys or a successful Lockpick roll. Once the
investigators have access to one, the walls are cardboard thin
and can be peeled back, allowing them to move between trailers.
Bazzledon’s Office
The curtains are lazily pulled in this office, letting sharp beams
of light cut through the dim, dusty haze. For the most part,
Bazzledon is never in his office; instead he is always on set,
shouting directions, making plans, and working to bring about
the final scene, the culmination of his plans.
In one corner, a tangled mess of bedsheets indicates the
location of a bed. Beside it, a large trunk full of clothes and a large
mirror takes pride of place. A cold iron rests beside it. In front
of the mirror, a half dozen vials of make-up, gels, and ointments
are neatly stacked. His desk is built out of an old train table and
it is covered in papers. The other side of the room contains two
comfortable looking armchairs, between them sits a small table
with a number of expensive looking liquor bottles on top.
Investigators can take many different avenues of investigation
while in this room, uncovering different information:
The papers on the desk are rather overwhelming and nothing
of value comes out on a quick glance through the dense sheets of
numbers and words.
- On a successful Accounting roll, investigators realize
that Bazzledon’s numbers don’t add up. There’s no
attempt to budget or forecast costs for the film. Whatever
reason he has for making this movie, it’s not about cash.

 A Spot Hidden roll uncovers a letter scrunched up and
tossed in the bin. It is from someone named ‘Fat Tony’

Bazzledon has moved up to Arkham and that if Tony’s

- Investigators looking into the scripts can use a
Language (English) or Library Use roll to sift
through the various drafts abandoned in a pile. It
becomes clear that earlier drafts of the script called for
the kidnapping to take place by human cultists rather
than frog-men. The frog-like additions appear to have
been added by Ginny Dubois.
- Anyone reading the correspondence discovers there was
a bidding war by various companies to sponsor the film.
While a major cough syrup company offered $6000 to
be paid on release, a new antacid company called Tum-
Eez offered Bazzledon $4000 upfront. He appears to
have chosen the lower bid and taken the money.
