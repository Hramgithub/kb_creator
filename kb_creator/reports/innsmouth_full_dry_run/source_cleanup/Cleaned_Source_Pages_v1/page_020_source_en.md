McGuire’s Lab

Wooden lab benches cover one entire length of this room, whilst
on the other side, spikes are interspersed along the wall with
heavy film roll hanging from them. Film is strewn just about
everywhere, like a drunken spider's web, and all the windows are
blacked out by thick curtains. McGuire’s main workspace is lit by
a pair of harsh incandescent bulbs without any lampshades. A
partially disassembled camera is in pieces on the workbench. An
investigator passing a Science (Engineering), or Electronic
Repair roll marvels at the quality of workmanship that has
gone into this camera. This truly is cutting edge technology. It
seems to use a prism to split the light in two and then there are
filters set up to record two identical scenes at the same time.

Though the lab may be messy, there’s not really anywhere to
hide in it, and none of his drawers are locked. While investigators
come across a wide variety of unsafe chemicals, they find no
hidden treasures.

On McGuire’s desk, they find a short map of a cave system.
Drawn using a ruler and many right-angles, it’s more of a
conceptual cave map. However, the map seems to lead....
somewhere.

This might look suspicious, but it’s actually just a guide on
how to reach the cave where the prison scene is going to be shot
later today. McGuire and Bazzledon explored the caves a few
days ago and have picked a great location. If the investigators
follow the map, they Stumble onto Set (page 21).

Keeper note: none of these chemicals burn well, and
the most dangerous of them is only a light acid. They are
however, toxic, light sensitive, and expensive. The film?
Nitrate based and incredibly flammable.
