On Set

During the initial investigation, everyone involved in the
production is working in a large open field beside the abandoned
rail tracks. The weather today is dramatic, with stormy clouds
threatening thunderstorms later, but for now there’s only a light
breeze blowing.

This is likely the first opportunity the investigators have had
to look over the dramatic landscape of Innsmouth. The valley
below looks to be one extended swamp, but rising up above the
far side of the town, jagged peaks and cliff faces give a feeling
of enclosure and isolation. It’s hard to imagine that this lost bit
of coastline is only a 45 minute bus ride from Arkham. Truly,
modern technology is a marvel.

P.A Bazzledon and Christopher McGuire are quietly arguing
over some technical points by the camera. Meanwhile, Ginny
Dubois is resting on a foldable chair under a giant mink fur coat
in a thick pair of sunglasses, while smoking from a cigarette
holder. Two larger members of the Crew are sitting on the
ground nearby, playing the dice game Twenty-Six. They are
half-dressed as ‘frog-creatures’: their legs are made up to look
scaly and wrong. Lying on the ground beside the Crew, there
is a sort of plastic mold that looks like it covers a person’s
torso, arms, and head, turning them into a plastic-y frog-like
creature. At first glance, it’s a terrifying twisting of plastic lying
haphazardly abandoned on the ground. It is only after the Crew
get up and begin preparing for the scene that investigators truly
comprehend it as a piece of costume.

Keeper note: while to a modern eye, the plastic costumes

may remind them of out-of-date B-movies or Doctor Who

reruns, to the public of the 1920s, elaborate costumes
are quite new and unusual, and the investigators may
genuinely be a little disturbed by it.

Investigators can stand and watch filming for a while, but
nothing of interest happens. Ginny is not a good actress,
hamming it up horrendously. And the scene they are shooting
is little more than her running slowly through the field while the
two lumbering frog creatures pursue her. Investigators passing
a Spot Hidden roll notice the reflection of some bit of light
from a set of rocks by the tracks on the way into town.

It seems that, while filming, no one has time for them on set.
Investigators may choose to investigate the rocks by the tracks,
heading to The Railway Entrance (page 18). They can instead
choose to wander down Into Innsmouth (page 22) and begin
asking questions there.

Other than that, the remainder of the set is an empty field.
The creaking cast iron structure of the old railway station is
about five-hundred yards away. Investigators notice there’s a
foreboding looking gap between the rocks by the tracks if they
head towards the station

Investigators initially approaching Bazzledon get a very brief
welcome and his deepest gratitude for agreeing to help ‘sort out
this little problem’. The man is charming but apologies and says
that the matter of the film must come first, similarly McGuire
brushes the investigators off, uninterested.

Investigators can approach Ginny before filming starts. She
assumes they are fans and of course declares that she will give
them her autograph. Bustling around, she demands they produce
pen and paper, taking charge and signing a rather intricate
signature with a heart over the i. Investigators interrupting this
little ritual, or clarifying that they are not fans suffer a penalty
die on any future Social roll with Ginny.

+ Investigators asking Ginny if she knows anything about
the Jewel are told that she has no idea where it could
have ended up. Maybe it’s just lost somewhere on the
train. That Diaz girl is a bit of an airhead after all.

+ If asked about Handout 2, Ginny doesn’t recognize
it, but worries that it might be one of her fans. Some of
them get a bit intense. Not like the lovely investigators,
who seem adorable.

+ If asked about the rest of the cast, Ginny is effusive with
her praise of Bazzledon, (“Such a great mind!”), a little
colder over McGuire (“Why can’t he get that damn
machine to work?”), but saves her most savage critique
for Diaz (“She just seems a bit uppity you know? Like
she doesn't know her place.”)

+ If asked about Innsmouth in general, Ginny marvels at
the quaint little town, saying it’s her first time here, but
she much prefers the hubbub of Arkham.

Ginny’s answers are overtly performative and anyone passing
a Psychology roll intuits that she likely knows far more than she
is telling about the Jewel, the note, or the town of Innsmouth.
But they don’t get any time to push her on details.

After asking Ginny a few questions, there is the sound of
whirring as McGuire fixes the camera and Ginny immediately
extricates herself from questioning and makes her way out in
front of the camera. The two Crew members begin to suit up.
and are suddenly terrifying frog creatures and Bazzledon cries
‘Lights, Camera, Action!

Keeper note: have the investigators met Ginny before

whilst playing through Stage Fright? Don’t worry about it.

She doesn’t remember them at all and treats them exactly

the same.
