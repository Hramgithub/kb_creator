INTO INNSMOUTH

Inevitably, at some point, investigators will start asking questions
around Innsmouth. They may simply begin asking after Sammy,

or they may wish to see if they can definitively connect Ginny

to Innsmouth. Investigators may instead begin looking to see if
they can uncover any cults or otherwise malevolent forces in the
town who may be interested in stealing the Jewel.

Innsmouth is an unfriendly town. Locals avoid going out
into the street if they see the investigators passing, and many
of the roads they walk through feel completely abandoned.
Investigators can’t help but feel like they are being watched
as they move through town and may occasionally spot queer
glowing eyes following them from darkened windows.

Whatever their approach, the Keeper can suggest The
General Store (adjacent) opposite The Gilman House
Hotel (page 12) as a first port of call.

Pete

The General Store

The General Store is a well-known chain and laid out precisely
the same as the equivalent shop in Arkham. Though this shop is
not quite as well stocked. The store brings back into focus how
thoroughly odd Innsmouth feels, giving the investigators a taste
of mundanity throughout all the chaos.

A single employee, a young man from Arkham named
Pete, sits behind the counter. He is cheery and friendly to the
investigators. He clearly doesn’t get much business and is glad
to see some new faces.

Pete is happy to gossip about Innsmouth and how weird it
is. However, he doesn’t actually know much about the town,
commuting to work from nearby Newburyport. Pete sees
Innsmouth as simply a backwater town, dying because it can’t,
or won't, adapt to modern times. While the fishing is good, the
little Innsmouth boats can’t compete with the modern trawlers
in the bigger ports. He says there was also some kind of disease
or epidemic a generation ago, and he thinks that’s why so many
people here look ‘kind of weird’. But you know, he’s paid hourly,
and it’s an easy job and no one bothers him or tries to convert
him to the local religion.

+ Pete doesn’t know anyone called Sammy or Ginny. He
confesses he barely knows anyone in town.

+ If the investigators are looking to get hold of some
alcohol, Pete smiles and politely directs them to a nearby
hovel, where some passable whisky can be bought from
an old lady with a fishy countenance. She is covered in
blankets.

+ Asked about Innsmouth more generally, Pete says that
he thinks the locals worship some kind of fish-god. It’s
a bit like Christianity, headquartered at the old Masonic
Lodge. He thinks the idea is that the locals worship
something that actually benefits them, like a god who
sends them more fish. He’s not one to judge.

+ If the investigators want to know more about Innsmouth,
Pete reckons their best bet is to talk to old Zadok
Allen. Zadok is a drunken old man, but is one of the few
residents of Innsmouth who seems social and interested
in talking. He pops in most mornings and talks Pete’s
ear off for half an hour before normally wandering off
looking to earn enough to buy a bottle of whiskey. On
most days, Zadok can be found at The Fire Station
(page 23), where he either sunbathes or shelters from
the weather.
